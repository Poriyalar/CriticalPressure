ΔPcrit · Critical pressure screening for wellbore brine leakage

Pressure increase in a deep injection zone required to sustain flow of native brine up an open wellbore to the base of a protected shallow aquifer. Formulations of Nicot et al. (2009, Energy Procedia 1:47–54) as adopted in US EPA Class VI AoR guidance and Bump & Hovorka (2023, IJGGC 129:103967). Brine density: Rowe & Chou (1970). Brine viscosity: Kestin et al. (1981).

Hydrostatic P(zi) minus measured initial reservoir pressure. Positive = underpressured injection zone: the deficit must be recovered before brine can be lifted, so it adds directly to ΔPcrit. Negative for overpressure.

Assumes an initially hydrostatic system (integrated numerically from atmospheric at the surface using local brine density) and an open wellbore whose starting fluid grades linearly in density from the upper-aquifer fluid at zu to injection-zone brine at zi. Depths are below sea level offshore; the geothermal gradient applies below the mudline. All salinities in ppm TDS (NaCl equivalent).

Governing equations

Critical pressure (Nicot 2009)

ξ = (ρ_i − ρ_u)/Δz    λ = (ρ_i − ρ_i,u)/Δz
Fast: ΔP = ½ g ξ Δz² + ΔP_deficit
Slow: ΔP = ½ g Δz (ρ_i,u − ρ_u) + ΔP_deficit

Pressure AoR (Theis / Warner & Lehr 1977)

ΔP(r,t) = Qμ/(4πkh) · E₁(r²φμc_t / 4kt)
AoR: r where ΔP(r, t_end) = ΔP_crit

Wellbore leakage (for ΔP_ex = ΔP_inj − ΔP_crit > 0)

Darcy plug: Q_w = k_w·πr_w²·ΔP_ex/(μΔz)
Open, laminar: Q_w = πr_w⁴·ΔP_ex/(8μΔz)
Open, Re>2100 (Blasius): v = [2ΔP_ex·D^1.25/(0.316·Δz·ρ^0.75·μ^0.25)]^(4/7)
Coupled: ΔP_ex = Q·R_in(t) + ΔP_wb(Q) + Q·R_out(t)
R(t) = μ·[½E₁(φμ_n c_t r_w²/4kt) + s]/(2πkh)
≡ μ·ln(r_e,eff/r_w)/(2πkh),   r_e,eff = 1.5√(ηt)

Salinity AoR (radial ADE, α_L = ε·r)

φ∂C/∂t + (Q_w/2πbr)∂C/∂r = (1/r)∂/∂r[rφ(α_L|v|+D_e)∂C/∂r]
u = r²/2,  A = Q_w/2πbφ,  σ = t·√(2(εA+D_e)A)
Const. Q: C = C_bg + (C₀−C_bg)·½erfc[(u−At)/√2σ]
Variable Q(t): finite-volume solve in u (van-Leer TVD)

ρ_i,u = injection-zone brine density evaluated at upper-aquifer depth (T, P) — i.e. thermally equilibrated after being lifted. The "slow" (thermal-equilibrium) case is EPA's preferred bound for locations far from the injector; the "fast" (instantaneous pulse) case keeps the invading brine at its in-situ injection-zone density. E₁ and erfc via Abramowitz & Stegun approximations. The salinity advective front sits at R(t) = √(2At).

Theis solution, ΔP(r,t) = Qμ/(4πkh)·E₁(r²φμct/4kt), for constant-rate injection into an infinite homogeneous single-phase aquifer (Warner & Lehr 1977; Nicot 2009 §4.3). The AoR radius is where the pressure front intersects ΔPcrit. Q is volumetric at reservoir conditions; brine viscosity at injection-zone T, P, S from Kestin et al. (1981). A multiphase reservoir model is required for accurate delineation — this is a screening estimate.

Constant ΔPinj. Leakage Qw = Cw·(ΔPinj − ΔPcrit,slow); zero below critical. Transport: vertically averaged radial advection–dispersion of TDS as a conservative tracer, with scale-dependent dispersivity αL = ε·r (field compilations of Gelhar et al. 1992 and Schulze-Makuch 2005 show αL grows ~linearly with transport distance; ε ≈ 0.1 is the classic screening value). This choice makes the mechanical dispersion coefficient constant in u = r²/2, giving a closed-form erfc solution validated against a TVD finite-volume solver (leading-edge front to <1%). The salinity AoR is where TDS crosses the threshold (set it above the upper-aquifer background).

Caveats: (1) Dense brine entering a lighter aquifer slumps and can finger; the vertically averaged model is justified for thin aquifers and overestimates lateral reach near the well otherwise — gravity-current/fingering treatment for thick aquifers is noted for future work. (2) Ambient groundwater flow is neglected: the plume is radially symmetric, whereas regional flow would skew it down-gradient. (3) With Couple to aquifers off, Qw ignores reservoir drawdown and the open-hole option is an upper bound; with it on (default for Darcy/open sources), a series-resistance balance — radial inflow from the injection aquifer, wellbore conduit, radial outflow into the shallow aquifer, skin on both open intervals — is solved by bisection. Each aquifer resistance is the transient Theis well function at the well radius, R(t) = μ[½E₁(φμnativectrw²/4kt) + s]/(2πkh), following the semi-analytical leaky-well literature (Nordbotten et al. 2005; Cihan et al. 2011) — equivalent to an effective influence radius re,eff(t) = 1.5√(ηt), the classic radius of investigation, so no arbitrary fixed re is needed and time enters only logarithmically. Injection-aquifer φ, ct are taken from the Pressure AoR tab; upper-aquifer φ is the transport porosity. The outflow prefactor viscosity is the leaking brine at upper-aquifer (T, P) (the near-well region is brine-saturated after breakthrough, cf. ρ_i,u); the E₁ diffusivity uses the native fluid. Because R(t) grows, Q(t) declines slowly; the transport model uses the volume-equivalent mean rate Q̄ = (1/t)∫Q dt′ (quasi-steady approximation to the van Everdingen–Hurst constant-pressure solution, good to a few % after early time). Coupling is first-order for open holes (~30–40× rate reduction; reservoir ends carry >99 % of the pressure budget, wellbore friction negligible) and negligible for Darcy plugs. Time-varying leakage is available in the companion Julia module (radial_salinity_solve) and, for a piecewise rate schedule, in the Salinity AoR · var rate tab.

Piecewise leakage-rate schedule: Q = R₁ on [0, t₁), R₂ on [t₁, t₂), then a ramp from R₂ to R₃ over [t₂, t₃] — linear, or exponential relaxation Q(t) = R₃ + (R₂−R₃)·e^(−3(t−t₂)/(t₃−t₂)) (reaches R₃ to within 5 % at t₃) — and Q = R₃ thereafter. The simulation runs 25 yr past t₃ so plume arrest and post-shut-in relaxation are visible.

Because Q varies in time the closed form no longer applies; this tab runs the same van-Leer TVD finite-volume solver as the Julia radial_salinity_solve (u = r²/2 coordinate; Dirichlet C₀ inlet while leaking, no-flux after shut-in). Aquifer thickness, porosity, ε, De and the TDS threshold are taken from the Salinity AoR tab; source/background TDS are the injection-zone/upper-aquifer salinities. Same caveats as the constant-rate tab (thin-aquifer vertical averaging; no ambient flow). Reservoir coupling does not apply on this tab: the rate schedule is prescribed directly rather than computed from a driving pressure — to get a coupled, pressure-driven Q(t), read Q̄ or Q(t) off the constant-ΔP tab (or the Julia module, which accepts t_yr per step) and enter it here.

Screening-level calculation only — assumes hydrostatic initial pressure (unless a deficit is specified), open wellbore, no thief zones, NaCl-equivalent salinity. Not a substitute for site-specific multiphase modeling.
